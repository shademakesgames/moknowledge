1. What approach did you take to scraping and structuring the knowledgebase data?

At first, I tried basic HTML scraping and tried with a variety of websites. I then
realized that a majority of small businesses use website builders like WIX, which
meant I had to change my method.

The current method fetches the homepage and goes two links out from the homepage. It
also has a hard cap of 40 per crawl. I decided this was a good balance of getting
lots of information, but also at a good pace. It is also able to read JavaScript
content (some of the websites have very little readable HTMLs).

For the knowledgebase, I made the scraper target all the needed information. I also
made it detect any legal policies / disclaimers. I also added an extra field to add
any competitor information. It then can be edited by the user. At the moment, some
fields return empty. I didn't implement this, but a good way to enter those missing
fields would be by using an LLM or a simple quiz to the user after. For now though,
they are just editable fields that can be altered by the user. The fields have review
tags so that they can keep track of what has been reviewed properly.

2. What information beyond our current baseline did you choose to include, and why?
I chose legal policies / disclaimers. This is because most of the websites did have a
legal website. I think it is important that we don't market anything that is 
contradictory to the legal policies of the company. 
While the scraper doesn't look for it. I added a field that the user can put in the
knowledgebase for competitors. This is so that MoFlo Cloud could scrape them and use that
information to help the user.

3. How would your knowledge base design improve the outputs of MoSocial, MoMail, and MoBlogs specifically?
I think that the outputs of the knowledgebase could be used to provide needed context to
the LLM that is running MoSocial, MoMail, and MoBlogs. With all these fields, a LLM
has more than just a website. it has the needed information to market the company accurately,
and quickly.

The knowledge library has view options for card and table, meaning you can keep the knowledges 
organized. The details section can also be toggled from the fields or expanded modes. Field allows
the user to look at what is missing, where as expanded makes it look like a document that
can easily be copy and pasted.

4. What would you improve or change about MoKnowledge if you had more time?
I would improve the scraper and implement LLMs to try to fill each field as accurately as
possible. Some of the websites I tried were still not able to get information. More fields
would also be good to get, like best posts across all socials.

I like the idea of adding visibility statistics (Tristan talked about this for an ad).
People could put there website in and get a score. I also think it would be cool
to add a predictive score. Like "This is your current score, but with MoFlo you could
be 10x more visible." You could do this by collecting visibility data from before your
clients used MoFlo and compare them to after.

You could also add a suggested marketing strategy. Feed the JSON to an LLM to tell you
the best marketing stratagies and even come up with some examples to get started.

5. What was the most challenging part of this assignment?
Websites don't follow a set standard. I had to update the parser a couple of times
and it is still arguably worse than what MoFlo has. Also, scraped information
was sometimes put in the wrong fields.
